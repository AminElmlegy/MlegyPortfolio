SUMMARY

From a messy group order to one shared cart

Problem

Group orders had no clear way to be managed, and splitting the bill and collecting payment was manual.

Solution

Make group ordering efficient, with automated bill splitting and payment collection.

Delivered

02 · RESEARCH

Listening before designing

Three methods, each answering a different question.

Stakeholder and user interviews

30 interviews on how people order as a group, why they use Jahez, what goes wrong with payment and what the journey feels like.

Competitive analysis

Five apps compared on pricing, offers, loyalty, restaurant range, reviews, delivery and customer support, to find market gaps.

Survey

256 replies on demographics and ordering and payment habits, to check interview findings against a wider group.

The pain point we designed around: splitting the bill and collecting payment from everyone in a group order.
03 · USERS

Meet Mahmoud

A 30-year-old engineer. His pain points came straight from the research: delayed orders, no easy way to split a bill and unreliable support.

We then mapped the full journey, from awareness to delivery, with actions, goals, feelings and pain points at every stage.

04 · IDEAS

Designing the group ordering flow

Card sorting with 10 participants shaped the app’s structure. Then we mapped the group order task step by step to find the shortest path for every member.

A STORY IN FIVE CHAPTERS

EGYFOOT

Inspired by Ao Ashi: one scout, one sharp eye, and one player nobody else had noticed. This is how EGYFOOT helps the scout find him.

Karim and the scout are story characters, made up to explain the design. The screens are real.

5story chapters, each with a problem and a solution
THE UX CHALLENGE

Make hidden talent easy to find, and easy to trust

Problem

Talent in Africa and MENA goes unseen. Scouts can’t be at every pitch, and players have no trusted way to show what they can do.

Solution

Short, verified clips become searchable profiles. Players get a simple habit loop, and scouts get fast search backed by evidence.

UX goals

Trust in the data. Speed for scouts. A daily habit for players.

01

Nobody is watching

Karim is 17 and plays for an academy in Cairo. He is fast, but nobody beyond his own pitch has seen him play.

Problem

Young players have no simple way to be seen beyond their own pitch, and don’t know where to start.

Solution

One clear first job: record a clip. A daily quest, XP and a streak turn it into a habit.

!
02

The clip

He films a dribble run on his phone. It lands in a vertical feed with a verified badge.

Problem

Scouts can’t trust random clips, and players need a format they already know how to use.

Solution

A vertical feed like the short video apps players use daily, with a verified badge and follow, like, comment and share.

03

The eye

The scout types what he sees in his mind: “explosive right winger under 18”. The hub ranks players for him.

Problem

Scouts can’t watch every academy, and rigid filters make searching slow.

Solution

AI search in plain language, ranked player cards, four tabs that mirror the real process, and a one-tap invite to trial.

ZOOM
04

Proof

A good scout trusts evidence, not guesses. Every stat links to the exact match moment.

Problem

Scouts don’t trust stats they can’t check.

Solution

Broadcast-style callouts on every clip, stats linked to timestamps, and scout-verified data.

The duel
